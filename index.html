Prototype writeup

What have we done so far?

We have createdWe extracted monthly near-surface air temperature (tas) and precipitation (pr) from the CMIP6 GFDL-ESM4 model for the historical run and three future scenarios (SSP1-2.6, SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5). We regridded them to 3,874 two-degree land cells and averaged each calendar month over 1995–2014 and 2081–2100. With this we have created an interactive visualization using D3: a map of temperature or rainfall change linked to a latitude profile on the same vertical scale. Readers can switch the emissions path, step through months or play them as an animation, and hover any cell or latitude for exact values. We currently have the months in shortened abbreviations but it may be changed for better readability. The color scales stay fixed across scenarios and months, so the colors can be compared fairly when the reader switches views. We also working on a method to potentially add a zoom feature on the map to see more detailed view of certain areas besides the local view. We have created a general layout to show a story by visualizations and the data that we have analyzed.

What will be the most challenging part to design, and why?

The main challenge that we will face is being able to show the data through the visualization without over cluttering. This design problem mainly comes from moving a single global number to the local view.. A second challenge is showing uncertainty transparently. Right now everything comes from one model, which sits at the low end of CMIP6 warming, and adding more models means deciding whether to show a range, a median or a model picker without cluttering the map. Rainfall is also tricky to encode: percent change exaggerates small changes in very dry months, but absolute change in mm/day hides big shifts in dry regions. Finally, the "your city" view depends on a matching method readers have never seen. We need an interaction that makes "the place whose climate today matches yours in 2100" feel intuitive rather than like a black box. This is an important part as we need to be able to relate the visualization and story to the reader without having them feel overwhelmed by the data.

Data: CMIP6 (NOAA public dataset on Google Cloud), GFDL-ESM4 r1i1p1f1, monthly tas and pr, regridded to 2° land cells. Place names: Natural Earth (public domain). Built with D3.js.
